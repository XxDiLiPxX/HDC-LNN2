1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CLOSING FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In this optimization phase, we systematically explored empirical threshold calibration
and statistical manifold covariance refinement for the HDC-LNN framework while
maintaining the underlying model weights and continuous representation frozen.

Threshold calibration proved highly effective and computationally free: shifting
from a rigid heuristic (mean + k*std on normal-only distributions) to an empirical
validation F1-maximizing operating point boosted test F1 by +0.1948 (0.6771 -> 0.8719)
on UNSW-NB15 and +0.1328 (0.6761 -> 0.8089) on KDD-test by resolving severe attack
under-detection (recall increased from ~52% to 80-85% while keeping precision high at
82-90%).

In contrast, refining the manifold estimation via Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance
and hidden-state standardization provided small precision gains (~0.5%) but confirmed
that covariance math alone cannot fix high-TPR false positive rates (FPR@95%TPR remains
locked at ~82% on UNSW and ~94% on KDD) due to an intrinsic ~45-47% geometric overlap
between benign traffic and stealthy attacks in the current 64-dimensional latent state space.

To achieve low FPR (<5%) at high threat capture (>95% TPR) in future work, architectural
representation changes are required: multi-scale continuous HDC encoding (SAX/RFF),
expanded liquid state dimensionality (d=128/256), and longer sequence context horizons.
